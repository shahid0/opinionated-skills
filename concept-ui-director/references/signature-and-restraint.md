# Signature And Restraint

Two artifacts. One decides what the product is remembered for. The other is
where most of the craft actually happens, and it is invisible unless written
down.

## The signature moment

**Exactly one** moment designed to be described to another person.

The currency is scarcity. Two signature moments means zero — attention does not
divide, it dissipates. A product with one memorable moment is remembered for it;
a product with five is remembered as busy.

It must be:

- **Derived from the concept**, not applied to it. If the moment would work
  equally well on another product, it is decoration.
- **On the path**, not beside it. A moment the person must go looking for is a
  feature. The signature is something they cannot avoid encountering while
  doing the thing they came to do.
- **Describable in one sentence** by a non-designer, without naming a
  technique. "The number stays put while everything else moves around it" is a
  moment. "Spring-animated parallax with depth blur" is a technique.
- **Repeatable without fatigue.** It happens every time. If it would be tiring
  at use fifty, it is wrong — either soften it or move the signature somewhere
  that is not on the daily path.

### Where signatures come from

- **The transition that explains something.** The tapped object becoming the
  screen. Causality made visible.
- **The moment of truth.** When the product tells the person the thing they
  came for. Usually the most under-designed moment in any product and the
  highest-yield place to spend.
- **The honest refusal.** Declining to show a number it cannot verify, in a way
  that reads as integrity rather than absence. Rare, memorable, unforgeable.
- **The single physical behaviour.** One element with weight, resistance, or
  momentum that behaves the way the concept implies matter should behave.
- **Restraint made visible.** A screen with one thing on it, when every
  competitor has twelve, is itself the moment.

### Where they do not come from

Splash animations, confetti, logo reveals, particle effects, anything that
happens before the person has done something. A moment the product gives itself
is not a moment the person receives.

## The restraint ledger

Editing is most of the craft. It leaves no trace in the output, so it does not
happen unless it has its own artifact.

Every cut gets a row: what was considered, why it was cut, and what would have
to change for it to return. Write it while designing, not after — a
reconstructed ledger is fiction.

```markdown
# Restraint Log

## Concepts not chosen
| Concept | What it would have made easy | Why not |
|---|---|---|

## Elements cut
| Element | Considered because | Cut because | Would return if |
|---|---|---|---|
```

The `Cut because` column has to name a real reason. "Not needed" is not a
reason. Real reasons look like: it competed with the hero, it would grow by use
ten, it implied certainty the data cannot support, it served us rather than the
person, it was inherited from a competitor rather than chosen.

### Why this file earns its keep

- It is how you audit the director's judgment rather than just its compliance.
  Reading the cuts tells you more about the quality of the thinking than
  reading the design does.
- It stops re-litigation. Six months later, when someone proposes the thing
  that was already cut, the reason is on record.
- It makes the density budget honest. Anyone can hold to a budget by never
  considering anything.

## Density and the cost of addition

Every element added takes attention from every element already present. This is
zero-sum, and treating it as such is the difference between restrained and
sparse-looking-but-cluttered.

Working discipline: an addition must name what it displaces. If nothing is
displaced, either the screen was underfull — possible, but rarer than it feels —
or the addition does not belong.

**One element shouts.** Per screen, one element holds primary visual priority.
Everything else yields, at every state. The exception worth knowing: on a screen
with no content yet, the hero is often the action rather than the display, and
the display should be absent rather than showing zeros.

## The test both artifacts serve

Ask, of the finished design: **what is the one thing?**

If the answer takes more than a sentence, the signature is missing or divided.
If the answer is a feature rather than an experience, the concept did not reach
the surface. If there is no answer, the work is competent and forgettable —
which is the only failure this skill treats as fatal.
